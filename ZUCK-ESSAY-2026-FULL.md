## The Core Argument: Distribution as Safety

The essay's central thesis: **The safest way to handle superintelligence is to distribute it to billions of people, not concentrate it inside institutions.**

### Three Philosophical Pillars

1. **Individual Empowerment as Source of Prosperity**
   - Superintelligence should empower individuals, not replace them
   - Personal agents managing health, career, finances, household

2. **Invention Over Automation**
   - Technology's purpose is creation and discovery, not displacement
   - "Invention rather than automation is what this technology is for"

3. **Balance of Power as Foundation of Safety**
   - No single system can be benevolent to everyone simultaneously
   - "There is no such thing as a singular benevolent superintelligence"
   - Competing agents check each other (like democracy/market)

### The "Alignment Is Flawed" Argument

Zuck's most controversial claim:
> "People's diverse values represent different tradeoffs they would make on important issues. There is no technological solution that can align with everyone's opposing interests and values at once... Any singular superintelligence would have to prioritize some values over others and in the process would be incapable of being benevolent to everyone."

**His solution:** Personalize models to individual/group values instead of seeking universal alignment.

## The Contradiction: Free Tier + Auction = Permanent Underclass

Zuck's vision promises:
- Free baseline compute for billions
- Paid premium via "dynamic auction"

**The Problem:** This creates a two-tier system where:
- Free tier = generic, limited capabilities
- Paid tier = auction-based, goes to highest bidder
- Result: The gap between rich and poor compounds in intelligence itself

This is the **permanent capability underclass** that Compute Fund v0 addresses.

## Critical Gaps in the Essay

### 1. Accountability Gap
- **Never addressed:** Who's liable when a personal agent serving User A harms User B?
- Balance of power theory answers societal distribution, not individual liability
- "Legal and safety boundaries" mentioned in passing, then abandoned

### 2. Concentration of Power
- Meta still controls: models, pricing, safety standards, release decisions
- "Open weights, closed spigot" — the infrastructure stays centralized
- Independent board = Meta appointees reviewing Meta products

### 3. Commercial Convenience
- Meta's business runs on: consumer hardware, glasses, open weights
- NOT enterprise API contracts (where OpenAI/Anthropic dominate)
- The argument maps perfectly onto Meta's commercial interests

### 4. China/Geopolitics Blind Spot
- 2024 essay had extensive China analysis
- 2026 essay: almost none — despite claiming distribution prevents adversary advantage
