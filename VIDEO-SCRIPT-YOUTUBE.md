## SCENE 2 — THE PROBLEM (0:30 – 2:15)

### Script

> "Let's be clear about what's actually happening. Compute is the new oil — except instead of countries fighting over it, a handful of companies already own the wells. Meta. OpenAI. Google. Anthropic. If you want to train a serious model, run serious research, or build something that matters, you go through them. They set the price. They set the rules. They decide what gets built.
>
> Zuck's essay is honest about the danger. He says: if powerful AI stays concentrated in a few institutions, you get a world where a tiny elite holds the intelligence, and everyone else is a customer. He calls for individual empowerment. He calls for invention over automation. He calls for balance of power.
>
> Then his plan... auctions off the compute. Free baseline for everyone, premium power for whoever bids highest.
>
> Think about what that creates. A student in Lagos with a great idea for a medical diagnostic — she gets the free tier, the same generic chatbot as everyone else. A hedge fund in Manhattan wants to run a frontier model all night — it just pays more. The gap never closes. It compounds. That's not empowerment. That's a permanent capability underclass with a nicer marketing slogan.
>
> And here's the part nobody says out loud: even the 'open' models run on their infrastructure. Open weights, closed spigot. Meta still controls the taps. One company — one board — decides who gets real power. The essay asks: who should control AI? And then answers: we will, but we'll be nice about it."

## SCENE 3 — THE VISION (2:15 – 4:45)

### Script

> "So what's the alternative? Not another company. Not a government. A fund. Owned by the people who use it.
>
> Compute Fund is a crowd-funded, decentralized compute platform. The idea is radical in its simplicity: the community owns the compute, and the community decides what it's used for. No single company. No corporate board. No benevolent dictator with a data center.
>
> Here's how it works — four layers, one ecosystem:
>
> **Layer one: The Baseline Layer.** Free compute for open-source projects. Anyone building in public — an open model, a research tool, a public dataset — gets a guaranteed floor of compute. Not a demo. Not a trial. A real allocation, so the things that benefit everyone can never be priced out of existence.
>
> **Layer two: The Merit Layer.** This is where the crowd decides. Projects are submitted to the community. People vote — with tokens, with compute credits, with reputation — on what deserves priority. A promising cure for a rare disease gets more compute than another chatbot. The market of people decides what gets built, not a product committee in Menlo Park. Good ideas get funded because they're good, not because they're profitable.
>
> **Layer three: The Commercial Layer.** Revenue-generating projects run on the platform and pay back into the ecosystem. A startup builds on Compute Fund, succeeds, and a portion of its compute spend flows back into the fund. The successful fund the next generation. This is how the ecosystem becomes self-sustaining instead of depending on one company's generosity — or one company's auction.
>
> **Layer four: The Reserve Layer.** The safety net. Emergency compute held in reserve — for a public health crisis, a critical open-source dependency that breaks, a researcher who needs surge capacity overnight. When something important happens and compute is needed *now*, the reserve answers. No ticket queue. No sales call. No waiting for approval from a corporate gatekeeper.
>
> And every layer is transparent. Every dollar in, every compute unit out, every receipt — public. When you fund a project, you don't just see a thank-you email. You see exactly what your compute bought: the model, the tokens, the output, the result. Proof you helped build the future."

## SCENE 4 — THE ANTIDOTE (4:45 – 6:20)

### Script

> "Now, here's why this matters right now. Zuck's essay says distribution is the safety strategy. We agree. But look at what he actually built: Meta still owns the models, Meta still prices the compute, and Meta's board still approves what gets released. He asks for balance of power — and keeps all the power. The *appearance* of openness without the reality of ownership.
>
> Compute Fund is the difference between a gated community with no fence and an actual commons.
>
> His model: one company sets the price, compute goes to the highest bidder, the rich get richer. Our model: the market of supporters sets the priority, compute goes to the most-backed projects, good ideas get funded regardless of starting capital.
>
> His model: a board decides what's safe to release. Our model: the community decides what's worth building — transparently, auditably, in public.
>
> His model: 'free AI' that funnels everyone into his ecosystem. Our model: open access, open ownership, open governance — no single point of failure, no single point of control.
>
> This isn't anti-Meta. It's anti-monopoly-of-mind. The essay asked the right questions: Who should hold powerful AI? Who decides what gets built? And its own answer was: *us, trust us.* Compute Fund's answer is: *everyone, prove it.*
>
> Zuck is right about one thing — the future IS for everyone. But a future handed down from above, priced by auction, is not a future for everyone. It's a future for whoever can afford it. The only way the future is actually for everyone is if everyone has a hand in building it."

## Production Notes

- **B-roll sources**: data centers (public domain/stock), GPU close-ups, community hackathons, open-source conference footage. Avoid any Meta/company brand footage beyond the essay quote (fair use, brief, critical commentary).
- **Music**: start tense/minimal (suspense strings + sub-bass), build through Scene 3 (percussion enters per layer), peak in Scene 4, resolve warm/uplifting in Scene 5.
- **VO delivery**: Scenes 1-2 measured and sharp; Scene 3 faster, enthusiastic, technical-but-accessible; Scene 4 slightly slower for contrast lines ("TRUST US" / "PROVE IT"); Scene 5 direct, warm, commanding.
- **Total runtime**: ~6:30 with pacing and beats. Cut Scene 3's layer explanations by ~15% for the 5:30 floor; extend Scene 4 with the Zuck-vs-Fund table for the 7:30 ceiling.
- **Accessibility**: burned-in captions throughout; on-screen text high contrast.
